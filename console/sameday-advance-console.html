Risk dials

The four dials below share 100 points between them — they say how much each type of evidence matters when scoring an applicant. Turn one UP and that factor counts for more (an applicant who's weak there gets hurt more, one who's strong gets rewarded more). Turn one DOWN and it barely affects the final score either way.

APPROVE CUTOFF
DECLINE CUTOFF

Approve cutoff: the score an applicant needs to hit for an instant yes — raise it and fewer people get auto-approved (stricter). Decline cutoff: the score below which it's an instant no — raise it and more borderline people get auto-declined instead of going to manual review. The gap between the two is the "manual review" zone.

Session Summary

Assessment engine, applicant simulation, and capital/profit modelling

12 August 2026

1. Assessment engine

Built a risk-scoring model across four weighted categories (income stability, account conduct, existing credit use, surplus buffer), all bank-data-derived — no manual applicant declarations.

Removing manual entry cuts applicant friction; scoring purely from bank data keeps the model honest and auditable.

Added two hard gates ahead of scoring: National Credit Code s6(1) compliance (term up to 62 days, fee up to 5%, interest up to 24% p.a.) and a payroll-eligibility check (same employer, consistent deposits).

s6(1) keeps the product outside regulated credit entirely; the payroll gate enforces the "wages only, no sole traders" policy.

Added a supplementary-income bonus (Centrelink / gig deposits, scaled by amount) and a 3-day post-payday buffer signal.

Rewards genuine financial headroom without ever substituting for the core wage/salary requirement.

2. Test population

Generated 100 simulated applicants across a realistic risk spread (20 strong / 55 average / 25 risky), later recalibrated so ~99% show a genuine salary/wage.

Lets you stress-test the assessment rules against realistic data before any real customer sees them.

3. Multi-year business simulation

Modelled applicant ramp-up, approvals/denials, available capital, funds outstanding, and repayments day-by-day, adjustable from 1 to 5 years.

Turns the assessment rules into a forward-looking view of cash flow, not just a per-applicant decision.

Added Australian company tax (adjustable rate, quarterly/annual), and full expense modelling — Tailfin bank-data fees, advertising, per-loan fees, fixed overhead.

Gets the model to genuine net profit after tax, not just gross fee revenue.

Modelled VIP repeat customers: no repeat Tailfin charge and funding priority for 1 year after first approval, at an adjustable repeat rate.

Reflects that bank-data access already covers a year; repeat business is materially cheaper to serve.

4. Capital-logic fixes

Fixed the funding gate to check against the maximum possible advance (not the average) before pulling bank data, so capital-short denials after paying Tailfin are now zero.

Stops paying for bank data on applicants the business can't actually fund — direct cost saving.

Replaced capital-short event tracking with "application-pause period" reporting — the day ranges and totals for when the site would show "not currently accepting applications."

Gives a concrete, plannable signal for when more capital is needed, ahead of time.

5. Denial-rate correction

Traced an initially very high denial rate to the payroll-eligibility gate (~32% of a stress-test population), separately from the score cutoff — then corrected the population so eligibility failures sit near 1%, in line with a realistic applicant pool.

The tool was flagging a data-modelling issue, not a genuine policy problem — now approvals reflect real risk, not an artefact.

6. Delivery

Renamed the project from Ledgerline to Same Day Advance throughout, and combined the assessment tester and simulation into a single two-tab HTML console, so a cutoff or weight change in one tab flows straight into the other.

One file, no build step — adjust risk appetite and see the 5-year capital and profit consequences immediately.

Added this Session Summary as a third tab in the same console, and renamed the output file to sameday-advance-console.html.

One link to share, one file to keep updated — no separate report to lose track of.

7. Repeat customers & repeat declines

Modelled VIP repeat customers: once approved, no Tailfin charge or reassessment for 1 year, and they get funding priority over brand-new applicants.

Reflects that the original bank-data access already covers a year — repeat business is genuinely cheaper to serve, not just assumed to be.

Added plain-language tutorial text under every heading and dial in the Assessment tab, explaining what turning each one up or down does to approvals.

Makes the tool usable by someone who isn't across the scoring mechanics, not just the person who built it.

Tested blocking reapplications from people already declined for not having a true wage/salary, for an adjustable number of months. Result: with the applicant pool now ~99% genuine wage earners, there are so few payroll declines to begin with that blocking only saves a few dollars a year at current settings (~$7/year), rising to a few hundred dollars over 5 years even under a generous 60% reapplication-rate assumption.

Worth having since it costs nothing to leave on, but it's not a meaningful lever here — the earlier fix to the applicant pool already removed most of the wasted spend this would have targeted.

Fixed a bug in how default rates were applied: the simulation was rolling the default chance on every single advance, including repeat/VIP ones, so a customer taking 10 advances in a year faced roughly 10x their real risk of ever being flagged as a default. Corrected it so the roll happens once per unique customer, on their first advance — a proven repeat customer no longer accumulates phantom risk just for borrowing often.

Bad debt now scales with how many unique people you lend to, not with how many times your best customers come back — which is what "default rate" is supposed to mean. This directly lowers reported bad debts in any scenario with meaningful repeat business, and makes the profit numbers more trustworthy.

Net effect Across the session, the tool went from a rough scoring sketch to a linked assessment-and-simulation console that shows, in one place, how a risk-policy change affects approvals, capital needs, and after-tax profit over a multi-year horizon — now with a built-in explanation of what each dial does, and a tested (if modest) answer on repeat-decline costs.
